THE FIRST HIT

Spider-Man wins every importance contest. The network barely notices.

Spider-Man has the highest degree, closeness, and betweenness in this snapshot. He is also the single removal that fragments the giant component most. Yet after removing him, 271 of 276 remaining characters—98.2%—still belong to one component.

The five pages left alone are Enigma, Ethan Edwards, Helix, Phil Grayfield (NFL SuperPro), and Thunderclap. Each had exactly one route into the giant component: Spider-Man. His enormous betweenness mostly reflects short routes through a hub, not a fragile bridge between two large worlds.

TRAFFIC ≠ BREAKAGE

Betweenness counts journeys, not wreckage.

A node scores high in betweenness when many shortest paths pass through it. Remove that node and those paths may simply detour. Fragmentation requires something stricter: a node must be an articulation point, the only connection between parts of the graph.

Only 13 of the 277 characters are articulation points. Hulk and Wolverine rank second and third in betweenness, but removing either one alone leaves every other character connected. Black Widow ranks eighth in betweenness yet causes the second-largest immediate split. Centrality and vulnerability overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

The busiest transfer station is not necessarily the bridge that collapses.
THE LONG ATTACK

Recalculation is where brokers pull ahead.

For a sequence of removals, yesterday’s ranking quickly goes stale. We therefore recompute the chosen score after every deletion. Adaptive betweenness pushes the largest component below half its original size after 63 removals; adaptive degree needs 74.

42largest component after 80 adaptive betweenness removals
107after 80 adaptive degree removals
191median after 80 random removals

At 80 removals, the broker strategy has reduced the surviving giant to 42 characters. Removing current hubs leaves 107 together. A random order usually leaves 191. The network is robust to accidents and isolated shocks, but not to an adversary who keeps finding the new routes between what remains.

WHAT THIS MEANS

Robust and vulnerable can both be true.

The first removal says the Marvel giant component has redundancy: even its biggest hub is not a single point of catastrophic failure. The full curve says that redundancy is concentrated. Repeated targeted removal eventually exhausts alternate routes far faster than random failure.

This is a structural experiment, not a claim about who matters in Marvel stories. Wikipedia links are editorial traces, the graph is a frozen roster, and deleting a page does not model a character leaving the fictional universe. The experiment answers a precise question: how connectivity in this particular graph responds to different node-removal rules.

THE QUESTION

When does a clearer picture become a different story?

Last week we removed Marvel’s busiest characters. This week we leave every philosopher in place and remove links instead. A backbone keeps the connections that stand out relative to each philosopher’s own pattern of references. It is a useful way to make a crowded network readable.

But readability has a price. Every link we hide is also a route we can no longer follow. We asked where that price becomes most visible: at which single filter threshold does the largest connected component lose the most members?

We checked every distinct edge-significance threshold, with tied links removed together. The biggest drop is 43 people, from 108 to 65, at α = 0.04712622658232835. It is a late fracture in a network that has already lost most of its routes.

THE TWO LINKS

Same source. Same weight. Same instant.

Augustine’s links to Erasmus and Nicolas Malebranche both have total weight 5, after summing references in both directions. Augustine has 96 neighbors and strength 158. From his endpoint, both links therefore receive exactly the same disparity score:

(1 − 5 / 158)95 ≈ 0.04712622658

A link survives if its score at either end is strictly below α. The other endpoints give larger scores—0.160140 for Erasmus and 0.054209 for Malebranche—so Augustine’s end sets the survival threshold for both.

As α crosses that value downward, the old 108-person component splits into groups of 65, 40, and 3. Augustine stays with Ambrose and Pope Gregory I. The largest fragment includes Aristotle, Plato and Thomas Aquinas; another includes Nietzsche, Leibniz and Locke. Those mixed memberships are a reason to resist calling the fragments clean historical schools.

THE COST OF CLARITY

The cleanest map hides the most people.

At α = 0.10, separate groups are easy to see, but 767 of 1,374 philosophers have lost every link. At α = 0.20, 950 still have a connection and the largest component contains 816. At α = 0.30, the largest component contains 1,052, and more of the original tangle returns.

For a single overview we would use α = 0.20 and state the losses in the caption. For an argument about connectivity, we would show the whole sweep. A tidy image alone cannot establish that traditions were disconnected.

The boundary is real in the filtered graph. The filter is part of the explanation.
WHAT SURPRISED US

Augustine becomes a bridge because we remove the detours.

Immediately before the largest fracture, only 273 of 9,139 links remain. More than three quarters of the original giant component’s philosophers are already isolated. The two Augustine links matter enormously in that sparse remnant; this experiment does not establish that they have the same role in the original network.

The surprise is how easily a visual choice becomes a structural claim. We start by wanting fewer lines. We end with islands, boundaries and a philosopher who appears to hold worlds together. All of those are measurable—but only after specifying exactly which graph we are measuring.

REPRODUCIBILITY

Every threshold. Every missing link.

We use the course’s frozen 15 September 2026 philosopher dataset: 1,444 nodes and 9,140 undirected links after summing weights in opposite directions. Analysis starts from its 1,374-node, 9,139-link giant component; the other 70 roster nodes are outside the scope. All 1,374 analysis nodes remain in every filtered graph, including isolates.

For endpoint i, degree ki, strength si and link weight w, compute (1 − w/si)ki−1 on the original weighted giant. Keep a link if the smaller endpoint score is strictly less than α. Scores are never recomputed after filtering. Degree-one endpoints have score 1 and cannot save their edge themselves.

We sort all distinct scores and add tied edges as batches, tracking component sizes with union–find. Reversing the sweep identifies the greatest absolute decrease in largest-component size. “Just above” includes the two boundary edges; “at the threshold” excludes them. Separate one-edge removals test their individual contribution. The published CSV records both sides of every threshold.

Overview colors use unweighted NetworkX Louvain (seed 0) on the original giant. The α = 0.30 backbone supplies fixed force layouts within each component (seed 413), with the largest component radially expanded (normalized radius raised to 0.4) for legibility and smaller components arranged around it; the 108-person close-up has its own pre-cut layout. The close-up colors eventual connected fragments, which are a different concept from communities. Layouts are illustrations, not evidence of distance or influence.

Reference counts at α = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30 and 0.50 match the course table. Independent browser calculations are checked against graph traversals, the original weights, and the saved fracture. These results concern a roster of English Wikipedia pages and editorial references; they do not measure the historical strength or direction of intellectual influence. Disparity scores are local null-model tail probabilities, without a multiple-testing correction.

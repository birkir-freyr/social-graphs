THE QUESTION

Does being the most visible make you indispensable?

Our starting question was simple: does the character everyone mentions also keep the network connected? An arrow from A to B means A’s Wikipedia article links to B’s article. It records an editorial reference within this roster, not a friendship, a fight, or an on-screen appearance.

Spider-Man receives links from 106 of the 303 pages — about 35%. His own article links to just nine other characters in the snapshot. That imbalance matters: recognition from other pages and links authored on a page measure different things.

03 / THE SHAPE OF ATTENTION

A long tail, hiding in plain sight.

Raw points show P(k): the fraction of pages with exactly k links. Red points average over degree ranges. Both axis modes use k + 1, keeping degree-zero pages visible. Empty degree counts are omitted only when the y-axis is logarithmic.

What exactly does the binning do?

In u = k + 1, bins have width 1 from u = 1 to 7, followed by [8,16), [16,32), [32,64), and [64,128). The count is divided by 303 and by the bin width. The point for [a,b) sits at √(a × (b − 1)), the geometric midpoint of its first and last included integers. Width-1 points coincide exactly with the raw probabilities; assertions check this in the analysis.

A straighter binned tail is a useful visual clue, not a fitted power law. There are only 303 observations, and other heavy-tailed distributions can look similar. We do not estimate an exponent from this plot.

04 / A REMOVAL EXPERIMENT

Take Spider-Man out of the picture.

We removed Spider-Man and all his incident edges from the main undirected component, then counted its connected pieces again. Of the 276 remaining characters, 271 still belong to one component. Five become isolated.

277connected before
→
271 + 5connected after + newly isolated

The hub reaches many pages, but most of those pages can still reach each other through other routes. This surprised us: high visibility does not make the entire network dependent on one character.

05 / THE MARGINS

A small island. Seventeen silent pages.

Nine characters form their own connected island with 22 directed links and 13 undirected pairs. Radian, Vyking, Scaredycat, and their neighbors can reach each other when direction is ignored, but no recorded link connects them to the 277-page main component. Select “The island” above to see the entire piece.

The remaining 17 characters have no incoming or outgoing links within the roster. An isolate is a statement about this dataset’s boundary: a page may have links elsewhere on Wikipedia, or meaningful relationships absent from the snapshot.

There are also 58 pages with zero incoming links and 20 with zero outgoing links. Neither number equals 17, because a page can have links in just one direction. Collapsing everything into one undirected degree would hide that distinction.

A blank space in the network is a finding about how we measured the world.
REPRODUCIBILITY

Follow the links. Check the numbers.

We used the course’s frozen 26 August 2026 snapshot. Every row of the node roster is added before any edges, preserving all 17 isolates. A directed NetworkX graph stores links; its undirected copy is used for connected components, the removal experiment, and the drawing.

There are 350 reciprocal pairs. Collapsing their two arrows into one pair gives 1,784 − 350 = 1,434 undirected edges, an average degree of 2 × 1,434 / 303 ≈ 9.47, and density ≈ 3.13%.

The analysis checks snapshot counts, endpoints, duplicate edges, self-loops, component sizes, bin normalization, and the removal result. The drawing uses a seeded force layout for the main component and a circle for the island; positions have no geographic meaning.
